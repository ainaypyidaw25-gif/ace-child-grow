## Required wording corrections before approval

1. Separate urgent symptoms from developmental concerns. Recommended English working copy:

   > Seek urgent medical assessment if your child suddenly stops using or moving an arm or hand, consistently neglects one side, has a sudden or significant loss of movement or ability, or loses a skill they previously had. If your child is not grasping or using both hands as expected, discuss it promptly with a qualified clinician and ask whether developmental screening is needed; do not wait for the next age checkpoint.

   A clinician must decide whether all skill loss should be described as urgent, and must define locally appropriate action and timing. Do not copy UK `GP/111/A&E` routing into Myanmar text.

2. Replace the current referral promise with:

   > A qualified clinician can assess the concern and decide whether developmental screening or referral to a pediatric or occupational-therapy service is appropriate. This guide is not a diagnostic or screening test.

3. Replace causal summary wording with the narrower everyday-use wording in the claim map.

4. Put the screening disclaimer before the observation questions, not only in `parentTips`.

5. Make the Myanmar safety threshold consistent. The indoor line currently says `မျိုမချနိုင်လောက်အောင် ကြီးသော` (too large to swallow), while other lines say `ပါးစပ်ထဲ မဝင်နိုင်လောက်အောင် ကြီးသော` (too large to fit in the mouth). The latter is the intended, more conservative threshold and should be used consistently.

6. Native clinical-language review must distinguish:

   - ordinary absence of a preferred hand from `လက်တစ်ဖက်ကို မသုံးခြင်း` or one-side neglect;
   - prompt developmental discussion from `အရေးတကြီး` urgent assessment;
   - occupational therapy from the current long descriptive phrase, using terminology families and Myanmar clinicians understand;
   - "fine motor" from the current title `လက်ချောင်းငယ် လှုပ်ရှားမှု`, which may sound literal rather than clinical or parent-friendly.

7. Keep all play optional and ability-led. Do not import the NHS hand-dominance PDF's older-child activities, force consistent hand use, or imply that a 13-18-month-old should already have a preferred hand.

## Required review

At minimum, record these as separate, named decisions against revision 5:

1. **Clinical/developmental review (`reviewScope=clinical`)** - a licensed pediatrician with documented child-development experience, preferably a developmental-behavioral pediatrician. Scope: urgency thresholds, regression and unilateral non-use, screening language, referral wording, feeding/choking implications, and whether advice is safe for Myanmar families.
2. **Fine-motor/OT review** - a licensed pediatric occupational therapist. Scope: age appropriateness of observation prompts and play, hand-preference age wording, bilateral hand-use distinctions, and avoidance of forcing dominance.
3. **Native Myanmar review** - a reviewer fluent in Myanmar health communication; urgent and referral text should be jointly cleared with the clinical reviewer. Scope: semantic parity, action urgency, family comprehension, and non-stigmatizing wording.
4. **Evidence/source decision** - record each of the four exact source records with an actual evidence status: `approved`, `in_review`, `awaiting_review` with a change note, or `retired`. Specifically resolve the renamed/redirected NHS PDF identity while preserving its undated status, and prohibit reuse of DBTH's UK-local access thresholds.

Approval must identify the reviewer, qualification, scope, exact reviewed revision, review date, next review date, and any limits. An education-only decision is insufficient for the red-flag and referral claims.
