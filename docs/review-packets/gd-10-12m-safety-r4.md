## Required wording and presentation decisions

These are blockers for exact-content approval, not permission for an agent to edit or publish:

1. **Separate the emergency contexts.** The current `redFlags[0]` combines head injury, poisoning/general breathing danger and an undefined “serious injury.” Prefer scenario-labelled bullets: a general emergency bullet for unconsciousness, absent/severely difficult breathing or seizure; and a head-injury bullet that preserves the NHS distinction between immediate signs (for example, failure to wake/stay awake, seizure or behaviour change) and urgent advice for any vomiting. Do not wait for repeated vomiting. A clinician must select the final signs and wording before a native-equivalent Myanmar edit.
2. **Validate the Myanmar emergency route.** Keep UK-specific `999`, `111` and `A&E` out of public copy. A locally practising clinician must confirm the usable Myanmar instruction (service/department/poison advice), including what to do where a single emergency number is unavailable.
3. **Fix section placement or labelling.** Bath and water-container advice currently lives in `data.outdoor[0]`. The rendered UI must present it as general water safety, not imply baths are “outdoor” advice.
4. **Keep layered drowning prevention.** Do not shorten the water wording to supervision alone. The broader guide must retain secure barriers plus close, constant, attentive supervision.
5. **Keep product claims in their own competence lane.** Stair-gate mounting, socket protection, removable plug covers, cupboard latches and cord hazards require the linked CPSC/product/electrical evidence scope; the four sources in this packet cannot approve them.
6. **Preserve non-diagnostic framing.** The two observations are prompts to notice hazards, not tests of development. Do not convert mouthing, pulling to stand, or cruising into pass/fail expectations.
